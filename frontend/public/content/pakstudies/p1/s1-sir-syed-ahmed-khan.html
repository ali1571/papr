REASONS FOR ALIGARH MOVEMENT? [7]

  • Sir Syed Ahmed Khan started the Aligarh Movement to modernize Muslim education. He recognized that Muslims were falling behind Hindus in civil service and army positions because they rejected Western education. The movement aimed to transform the medieval Muslim mindset, encourage adoption of Western education, and enable Muslims to compete for government positions, hence improving their socio-economic status in British India.
  • Following the War of Independence in 1857, Muslims faced severe political, social, and educational degradation. The British, believing Muslims were responsible for the uprising, punished by discriminating them. Muslims were removed from positions of power, marginalized in government services, and their community faced widespread persecution. The Aligarh Movement was established to help Muslims overcome this period of crisis and rebuild their standing in society.
  • The Movement aimed to bridge the growing divide between British authorities and the Muslim community. After 1857, British authorities distrusted Muslim loyalty, while Muslims viewed the British as foreign invaders. Sir Syed recognized that improving these relations was crucial for Muslim advancement and survival under British rule. Through the Aligarh Movement, he sought to create understanding between the two communities and demonstrate Muslim willingness to embrace modern education and progress.

why sir syed wanted improve relations between muslims and British? [7]

  • Sir Syed recognized that British rule was firmly established and would continue for the foreseeable future. Given the harsh measures taken against Muslims after 1857, including land confiscation and mass executions, he understood that Muslim survival and advancement required them to be on good terms with the British.
  • The deteriorating Muslim-British relationship had allowed Hindus to gain significant political and economic advantages. While Muslims remained alienated from the British, Hindus secured political rights and economic opportunities. Sir Syed realized that without improving relations with the British, Muslims would continue to fall behind as Hindus prospered.
  • Sir Syed worked to transform both British and Muslim attitudes. He sought to convince the British that their view of Muslims as disloyal and backward was incorrect, while simultaneously persuading Muslims to stop viewing the British as mere invaders. He believed that changing these perceptions was essential for Muslims to access British patronage and opportunities, particularly in education and civil service.

SIR SYED KHANS CONTRIBUTIONS [14]

A. Sir Syed Ahmed Khan’s Attempts to Improve Relations

1. Writing 'Causes of the Indian Revolt' (1858):

Sir Syed wrote this pamphlet to explain the reasons behind the 1857 War of Independence, highlighting British administrative failures rather than blaming Muslims alone e,g lack of indian role in the govt and forceful indian conversions to chirstianity. He argued that both Hindus and Muslims participated in the revolt and that many Muslims remained loyal to the British. This book was sent to high officials in british parliament and helped reduce British hostility towards Muslims.

2. Authoring 'The Loyal Mohammadans of India' (1860):

In this book, Sir Syed listed Muslim families who had saved British lives during the 1857 revolt, proving Muslim loyalty. He urged the British to end discrimination against Muslims and to consult them in governance, fostering better relations.

3. Explaining the Term 'Nadarath':

Sir Syed clarified that the term 'Nadarath,' used by Muslims for the British, derived from the Arabic word 'Nasir' (helper), not as an insult. This helped remove misunderstandings and improved mutual respect.

4. Writing 'Tabyin-ul-Kalam' (1862):

This book highlighted similarities between Islam and Christianity, promoting religious harmony. By showing shared values, Sir Syed aimed to reduce hostility and build bridges between Muslims and the British. in the book he translated bible and compared it with quranic teaching.

B. Sir Syed Ahmed Khan’s Efforts for Western Education Development.

1. Founding the Ghazipore Scientific Society (1863):

Sir Syed established the Scientific Society in Ghazipur to translate Western scientific works into Urdu, making modern knowledge accessible to Muslims. This initiative aimed to bridge the gap between traditional Islamic education and Western scientific advancements.

2. Establishment of Schools:

He founded several schools, such as Gulshan School in Muradabad (1859) and Victoria School in Ghazipur (1863), to provide modern education to Muslims and prepare them for competitive opportunities.

3. Creation of Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College (1875):

Sir Syed’s most significant achievement was the establishment of the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College in Aligarh, which later became Aligarh Muslim University. The institution offered both Western and Islamic education, producing future leaders like Liaquat Ali Khan and Ayub Khan.

4. Promotion of Urdu and Western Knowledge:

Through the Aligarh Institute Gazette, he spread scientific knowledge and fostered goodwill between Muslims and the British. His efforts encouraged Muslims to embrace Western education while staying rooted in their cultural and religious identity. he setup urdu journal, Tahzib ul ikhlaq in 1870 to spread awareness on modern subjects. he actually gave opportunities to emerging writers, and poets, to talk and share articles about various subjects to upgrade thinking of muslims.

C. political contributions

1. Advocacy of the Two-Nation Theory:

Sir Syed was the first to articulate the idea that Hindus and Muslims were two distinct nations with separate cultures, traditions, and interests. This concept later became the foundation of the Pakistan Movement and the demand for a separate Muslim homeland.

2. Protection of Urdu Language:

During the Hindi-Urdu Controversy (1867), Sir Syed defended Urdu as a symbol of Muslim identity and culture. He opposed Hindu demands to replace Urdu with Hindi as the official language, which reinforced the cultural divide between the two communities.

3. Opposition to Congress and Hindu Dominance:

Sir Syed advised Muslims not to join the Indian National Congress, fearing Hindu domination and exploitation. He believed that democratic systems would marginalize Muslims due to their minority status, leading him to advocate for separate political representation for Muslims.

4. Establishment of Political Organizations:

He founded the United Patriotic Alliance (1888) and the Muhammedan Defence Alliance (1893) to protect Muslim political rights and provide a platform for discussing their issues. These organizations laid the groundwork for future Muslim political mobilization.
