Islamic Reformers & Movements

Shah Waliullah

  • Real name was Qutb-ud-din bin Abdul Rahim.
  • Born in 1703 and passed away in 1762.
  • Islamic scholar and reformer who was born during the decline of Muslim power in the subcontinent.
  • Invited Ahmad Shah Abdali (Durrani) to India to fight and defeat the Marathas.

Syed Ahmed Barelvi

  • Born in Rai Bareilly near Lucknow in 1786.
  • Founded the Jihad Movement to free Muslims from oppression.
  • Student at Madrassah-e-Rahimiya, inspired by Shah Waliullah’s sons who taught there.
  • A man of action rather than just a scholar; took up arms against Muslim oppression.
  • Killed in the Battle of Balakot in 1831.

Jihad Movement

  • Initiated by Syed Ahmed Barelvi to liberate Muslims from non-Muslim oppression and restore Muslim power.
  • Inspired by Shah Waliullah’s teachings; led military campaigns against the Sikhs, achieving temporary success in Peshawar.
  • Organized an armed struggle in Punjab, gathering a huge army of 80,000 Mujahideen from across the subcontinent.
  • Syed Ahmed joined the army of Amir Khan where he learned European weaponry and military tactics.

Haji Shariatullah

  • Born in Bengal in 1781; passed away in Dhaka in 1840.
  • Initiated the Faraizi Movement in Bengal to liberate lower-class Muslims.
  • Emphasized observing Islamic Faraiz (obligations); his movement was both religious and social.
  • Opposed by Hindu landlords and the British elite, who eventually forced him to leave the region.

Faraizi Movement

  • A major Islamic reform movement in Bengal started by Haji Shariatullah.
  • Focused on uplifting artisans, poor craftsmen, workers, and peasants.
  • Aimed to purify Islam from non-Islamic/Hindu practices that had crept into the daily lives of Muslims.

Titu Mir

Real Name: Syed Mir Nisar Ali (1782–1831)

  • Bengali Muslim freedom fighter and Islamic preacher.
  • Organized poor Muslim farmers and weavers in Bengal against unfair taxes imposed by Hindu landlords and the British.
  • Led an armed rebellion in 1831, building a famous bamboo fort to resist British forces before being defeated.

Decline of Mughals & Early British Era

Zamindars

  • Landlords and tax collectors during the Mughal Empire who belonged to the wealthy and influential nobility.
  • Controlled large areas of land and some maintained private armies, playing a key role in local administration.
  • After Aurangzeb’s death, they refused to pay taxes or revenues to the central government in Delhi.
  • Frequently rebelled against central Mughal authority, severely crippling the empire’s financial stability.

Nadir Shah’s Invasion (1739)

  • Persian ruler Nadir Shah invaded the Mughal Empire in 1739, sacked Delhi, and looted immense wealth.
  • Stole famous treasures, including the Peacock Throne and precious jewels.
  • Exposed the complete military weakness of the Mughal Empire and shattered its prestige.

Ahmad Shah Abdali’s Invasions (1748–1767)

  • Afghan ruler who invaded India multiple times between 1748 and 1767.
  • Defeated the Marathas at the historic Third Battle of Panipat (1761).
  • Captured Kabul, Peshawar, Lahore, and Kashmir, but failed to consolidate rule and returned to Afghanistan.
  • His invasions further weakened Mughal authority, allowing regional powers (Marathas and Sikhs) to rise.

Robert Clive

  • British officer who established East India Company (EIC) political and military dominance in India.
  • Led British forces to victory in the Battle of Plassey (1757), defeating Nawab Siraj-ud-Daulah.
  • Appointed Governor of Bengal; faced accusations of corruption and plundering Indian wealth.
  • Struggled with opium addiction and personal issues, dying by suicide in 1774.

Warren Hastings

  • First Governor-General of British India, serving from 1772 to 1785.
  • Consolidated British rule through extensive administrative and military reforms.
  • Signed the Treaty of Salbai (1782), ending the First Anglo-Maratha War.
  • Faced accusations of corruption and mismanagement during his tenure.

Tipu Sultan

Known as the "Tiger of Mysore"

  • Ruler of Mysore (1782–1799) and son of Hyder Ali; a fierce opponent of British expansion.
  • Allied with the French and secured victory in the Second Anglo-Mysore War (Treaty of Mangalore, 1784).
  • Ceded half his territory after the Third Anglo-Mysore War (1789–1792).
  • Defeated and killed defending his capital, Seringapatam, in 1799 during the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War.

Black Hole Tragedy

  • In 1756, Nawab Siraj-ud-Daulah of Bengal captured Fort William in Calcutta.
  • Around 146 British prisoners were locked in a small, cramped, unventilated room.
  • Due to extreme heat and lack of air, 123 prisoners died overnight from suffocation.
  • Only 23 survivors were found the following morning.

Ranjit Singh

Known as the "Lion of Punjab"

  • Founder and ruler of the Sikh Empire in the early 19th century; crowned at age 21.
  • Expanded territory across Punjab, Kashmir, and parts of Afghanistan using European-trained troops.
  • Signed the Treaty of Amritsar (1809), maintaining a peaceful alliance with the British.
  • His death in 1839 caused instability, leading to the annexation of Punjab after the Anglo-Sikh Wars.

War of Independence (1857)

War of Independence Overview

  • Began in May 1857 as an armed uprising between Indian soldiers/rulers and the British.
  • Also known as the Sepoy Mutiny, Indian Mutiny, or the Revolt of 1857.
  • Started in the garrison city of Meerut and rapidly spread across Northern and Central India.
  • Effectively suppressed by the British within months, ending officially by August 1857.

Nana Sahib / Battle of Kanpur

  • Maratha leader who led the 1857 rebellion forces in Kanpur (Cawnpore).
  • Trapped British soldiers and civilians for over 3 weeks; granted safe passage but opened fire on them.
  • Responsible for the massacre of around 300 women and children at Kanpur.
  • Escaped into the jungles of Nepal after the British recaptured Kanpur, where he died.

Rani of Jhansi

Real Name: Rani Lakshmibai

  • Her state of Jhansi was annexed by the British under the Doctrine of Lapse (no male heir).
  • Joined the 1857 revolt, assisted by military commander Tatia Tope.
  • Died fighting bravely dressed in male warrior clothes during the Battle of Gwalior (1858).

Mangal Pandey

  • Indian sepoy who played a catalyst role in initiating the 1857 rebellion.
  • First sepoy to attack a British officer in Meerut over the introduction of greased cartridges.
  • Arrested, court-martialled, and executed (though briefly freed by fellow soldiers beforehand).

Bahadur Shah Zafar

  • The last symbolic Mughal Emperor of India.
  • Nominal leader of the Indian rebels during the 1857 War of Independence.
  • Surrendered to the British after the defeat; his three sons were shot dead, ending the Mughal dynasty.
  • Exiled to Rangoon, Burma (Myanmar), where he passed away in 1862.

Sir Syed Ahmed Khan & Aligarh Movement

Sir Syed Ahmed Khan

  • 19th-century Muslim reformer born in Delhi in 1817; worked as a judge under British rule.
  • Founded the Aligarh Movement to promote modern scientific education among Muslims.
  • Established the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental (MAO) College in 1875 (later Aligarh Muslim University).
  • Worked tirelessly to reconcile relations between the British government and Indian Muslims.

Aligarh Movement

  • Educational and socio-political movement started by Sir Syed Ahmed Khan post-1857.
  • Aimed to modernize Muslim society by encouraging Western scientific education.
  • Centred around the establishment of MAO College in 1875.
  • Successfully restored Muslim socio-political status and confidence in British India.

Book: 'Causes of the Indian Revolt' (1858)

  • Pamphlet written by Sir Syed to explain the true reasons behind the 1857 uprising.
  • Blamed British administrative errors (lack of Indian representation, forced religious conversions) rather than Muslims.
  • Proved that both Hindus and Muslims participated, and many Muslims remained loyal to the British.
  • Distributed to British Parliament members, helping reduce British hostility toward Muslims.

Book: 'The Loyal Mohammadans of India' (1860)

  • Written by Sir Syed Ahmed Khan to highlight Muslim loyalty to the British government.
  • Detailed stories of Muslim families who risked their lives to protect British citizens during the 1857 revolt.
  • Urged the British administration to end discrimination against Muslims and consult them in governance.

Two-Nation Theory

  • Concept first articulated by Sir Syed Ahmed Khan, stating Hindus and Muslims were two distinct nations.
  • Based on fundamental differences in religion, culture, social values, and political outlooks.
  • Warned that majority-rule democracy in India would permanently subjugate the Muslim minority.
  • Formed the ideological foundation of the Pakistan Movement leading to the 1947 Partition.

Promotion of Regional Languages (Post-1947)

Development of Sindhi

  • 1948: Sindhi Literary Board established, publishing numerous books and magazines in Sindhi.
  • Key literary figures include Pir Ali Muhammad Rashdi, Faqir Nabi Buksh, and G. Allana.
  • 1954: Bazm-e-Talib-ul-Muala set up to promote modern Sindhi literature.
  • Sarmast Academy established to honor famous Sufi poet Sachal Sarmast.

Development of Balochi

  • Radio Pakistan Karachi began broadcasting regular Balochi language programs after 1947.
  • Balochi Literary Association formed, publishing weekly and monthly periodicals.
  • Quetta Radio and TV station established dedicated Balochi broadcasts.
  • Promoted by prominent writers/poets like Atta Shad, Ishaq Shamin, Gul Khan Nazir, and Azad Jamal Din.

Development of Punjabi

  • Rich history in Sufi romantic folk poetry which maintained its popularity across Punjab.
  • Post-1947, government supported organizations dedicated to preserving Punjabi culture and language.
  • Radio, television, and film media actively promote classical literature and folk music.
  • Punjabi stage theatre gained immense popularity and official state backing.

Development of Pashto

  • Pashto literature gained momentum as Pashtun poets played a key role in the independence movement.
  • Peshawar University established as a major center for Pashto language research.
  • 1954: Pashto Academy established; created a widely accepted standard dictionary.
  • Post-graduate degree courses in Pashto literature introduced at university level in KPK.
