HIS CONTRIBUTIONS

  • Revival of Islam through Quran Translation: Shah Waliullah translated the Quran into Persian, making it accessible to Muslims in the subcontinent who couldn’t understand Arabic. This helped revive Islamic teachings and unity among Muslims.
  • Writings and Teachings: He wrote 51 books in Persian and Arabic, such as Hujjat Allah al-Baligha and Izalat-ul-Akhfa, to simplify and spread Islamic principles. He also emphasized unity between Shia and Sunni Muslims and corrected un-Islamic practices.
  • Madrassa-e-Rahimiya: Through his teachings at Madrassa-e-Rahimiya, he trained future leaders and missionaries. e.g syed ahmed shaheed barelvi.
  • Political Influence: He united Muslims against external threats like the Marathas, persuading Afghan ruler Ahmed Shah Abdali to defeat them in the 1761 Battle of Panipat.
  • Shia sunni: he wrote books on the 4 caliphs of islam in a way acceptable to shia and sunnis both, hence tried to unite both sects, so that a unified islamic front will stop marathas expansion.
  • Economic Reforms: He emphasized social justice, fair wealth distribution, and the importance of valuing laborers and craftsmen. He urged traders to adopt fair Islamic trading practices, eliminating corruption and ensuring just payment for workers.

why did syed ahmed brelvi had such a major influence on the revival of islam in the sub-continent? [7]

  • Leadership and Knowledge: Syed Ahmed Shaheed Barelvi was a highly learned Islamic scholar with deep knowledge of the Quran and Islamic principles. His piety and dedication to improving the spiritual and intellectual lives of Muslims earned him widespread respect and support.
  • Jihad Movement: He launched the Jihad Movement to liberate Muslims in Punjab and the North-West Frontier from oppressive rulers like Ranjit Singh, who banned Islamic practices such as prayer. His struggle aimed to restore religious freedom and revive Islamic rule.
  • Uniting Force: The Jihad Movement united Muslims under a common cause, inspiring them to fight for religious and spiritual freedom. Many of his followers were spiritual leaders, and their willingness to sacrifice their lives for Islam inspired future generations.
  • Legacy and Martyrdom: Syed Ahmed’s martyrdom in the Battle of Balakot (1831) solidified his influence as a symbol of resistance and revival. His movement laid the foundation for future Muslim uprisings and even inspired the Pakistan Movement, as he envisioned a state based on Islamic principles.

REASONS FOR THE FAILURE OF JIHAD MOVEMENT [4][7]

  • Lack of Military Training: Syed Ahmed’s army consisted mostly of teachers and spiritual leaders who lacked proper military training and tactics. This made it difficult to compete with the well-trained and well-equipped Sikh forces.
  • Internal Disunity: The Muslim forces were composed of individuals from diverse backgrounds, leading to mistrust and arguments. The Sikhs exploited this by spreading propaganda that Syed Ahmed’s forces were not true Muslims, further weakening unity.
  • Pathan Resistance: The Pathan tribes, who were part of the movement, were unwilling to compromise or accept leadership from outsiders. Their lack of cooperation and readiness to support the campaign weakened the overall Muslim forces.
  • Unpopular Taxation: Syed Ahmed imposed taxes on the local population to fund the military campaign, which caused resentment and loss of support among the people. This further weakened his position and the movement’s effectiveness.

Why did Syed Ahmed Shaheed Barailvi wish to revive Islam in the sub-continent? [7]

  • The first reason was the decline of Muslim political power and religious practices during his time. After the Mughal Empire weakened, Muslims had moved away from true Islamic principles. Many had adopted non-Islamic customs and traditions. Syed Ahmed believed Muslims needed to return to fundamental Islamic teachings to restore their lost glory and spiritual identity.
  • The second reason was the growing influence of Sikh power in Punjab and Northwest frontier regions. Sikhs had established control over Muslim-majority areas and were restricting Islamic practices. Muslims were being forced to abandon their religious obligations. Islamic institutions were being suppressed. This situation motivated him to start a movement for Islamic revival.
  • The third reason was the widespread adoption of un-Islamic practices and innovations (bidah) among Muslims. Many Muslims were incorporating Hindu customs and local traditions into their religious practices. These included visiting shrines for intercession and believing in supernatural powers of holy men. Syed Ahmed saw these as corruptions of pure Islamic teachings and felt it was essential to restore original Quranic principles.

why did haji had such a major influence on the revival of islam? [7]

  • Focus on Depressed Muslims: Haji Shariatullah targeted the most marginalized sections of Muslim society, such as farmers and artisans, urging them to abandon un-Islamic practices and strictly follow Islamic duties (Faraiz). This resonated deeply with oppressed Muslims.
  • Opposition to Hindu and British Influence: He fought against the cultural and economic oppression of Muslims by Hindu landlords and British indigo planters. His movement aimed to purify Islamic practices from Hindu and British influences, restoring Islamic identity.
  • Faraizi Movement: Haji founded the Faraizi Movement to revive Islamic principles and unite Muslims against non-Muslim rule. He declared India Dar-ul-Harb (land of war), where Islamic practices like Friday and Eid prayers were restricted, motivating Muslims to fight for their rights.
  • Socio-Economic Impact: The movement not only was to revive Islamic teachings but also addressed socio-economic issues, empowering Muslims who were demoralized by poverty and lack of opportunities under British and Hindu dominance.

HAJI SHARIATULLAH CONTRIBUTION

  • Spiritual Revival: Haji Shariatullah’s Faraizi Movement aimed to purify Islamic practices by eliminating Hindu influences and reviving strict adherence to Islamic duties (Faraiz). This brought about a spiritual awakening among Muslims in East Bengal.
  • Political and Economic Awareness: The movement united oppressed Muslim peasants against the exploitation by Hindu landlords and British indigo planters. It raised political awareness and encouraged Muslims to resist unfair taxes and laws, such as the ban on cow slaughter during Eid.
  • Social Unity and Resistance: The Faraizi Movement fostered unity among Muslims, leading to organized resistance against oppressive landlords and moneylenders. This laid the groundwork for future political movements, including the demand for a separate Muslim homeland.
  • Militant Leadership under Dadu Miyan: After Haji’s death, his son Mohsin-ud-Din (Dadu Miyan) expanded the movement, adopting a more militant approach. He organized armed resistance, boycotted taxes, and even attempted to establish a parallel Muslim government in East Bengal. he divided the region into circles under khalifas, to improve social and spiritual welfare of people. Dadu miyan died 1860.
  • Legacy and Decline: Despite its decline after Dadu Miyan’s imprisonment and death, the Faraizi Movement left a lasting impact by empowering Muslims, reviving Islamic identity, and inspiring future struggles for Muslim rights and independence.

TITU MIR

Titu Mir (1782–1831), whose real name was Syed Mir Nisar Ali, was a Bengali Muslim freedom fighter and Islamic preacher

He led an armed rebellion in 1831, building a bamboo fort to resist British forces but was defeated.

Titu Mir organized poor Muslim farmers and weavers in Bengal against unfair taxes by Hindu landlords and the British.

Explain how Titu Mir protected the interests of Muslim farmers in the subcontinent in the early nineteenth century? [7]

  • Titu Mir traveled across Bengal and witnessed the harsh conditions faced by Muslim peasants, who were burdened with excessive taxes (such as the beard tax) and forced labor by Hindu zamindars and British officials. He spread awareness about these injustices, using religious sermons to unite farmers against oppression. By highlighting their struggles, he gave them a shared cause to resist exploitation.
  • To fight back, Titu Mir organized a rebellion in the 1830s, mobilizing thousands of Muslim farmers. He encouraged them to refuse unfair taxes, such as the beard tax imposed on Muslims. His movement turned into an armed resistance, where his followers attacked oppressive zamindars and British officials. This rebellion was one of the earliest challenges to British rule in India
  • To protect his followers, Titu Mir built a bamboo fort in Narkelberia (near Calcutta) and trained a small army of farmers. This fort became a symbol of resistance, where Muslims could live freely without paying illegal taxes. Though the British eventually destroyed the fort with cannons
